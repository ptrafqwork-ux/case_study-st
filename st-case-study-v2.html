The assignment

ST's portal had the content. It didn't have a path from what users needed to where that content lived. Years of unstructured additions had turned the site into an org chart that happened to be a website. I rebuilt the micro IA, content relationships, and taxonomy — then stayed through Drupal implementation to make sure it held.

04 / Constraint / Mid-project reset

The direction changed. The date didn't.

February reset No timeline extension / Launch: 1 Apr 2026

A leadership change in February replaced the user-oriented IA I'd built with a corporate-structured mandate. The IA had to be rebuilt from the new direction — without moving the launch date.

Initial direction

The first IA grouped content by what users actually searched for — licensing, billing, safety. User-oriented structure.

Leadership change

New leadership wanted the portal to reflect the organization's divisions — Electricity, Gas, Sustainability — not user tasks.

What moved

IA, taxonomy, content mapping, and all downstream design work. Everything except the audit itself had to be reworked.

07 / Solution / UI reinforced the architecture

The interface made the structure visible.

The IA decisions only hold if the UI makes the hierarchy obvious. Four principles guided every page layout and component choice.

01

Horizontal cards

Scannable rows that show one decision per line, replacing stacked vertical cards that hid content below the fold.

02

Stronger CTA treatment

Primary actions got visual weight — filled buttons and contrast backgrounds — so users always knew the next step.

03

Focused landing pages

Each topic landing shows only its children. No cross-links, no sidebar promotions, no competing navigation.

04

One job per inside page

Detail pages do one thing: present the content and its CTA. Reference stays in tables, downloads stay in cards.

11 / Reflection / What changed in my practice

The system only holds when someone holds the thread.

11.1 What I'd do differently
  • Push for a content freeze earlier. Content was still being revised while I was mapping it. A formal freeze point would have saved a week of rework.
  • Document the IA rationale per section, not just the structure. When the direction changed, I had to re-explain why each grouping existed. Written reasoning would have made the pivot faster.
  • Build taxonomy earlier in parallel with IA. Waiting for IA sign-off before starting taxonomy meant the CMS team had less time to implement.
11.2 What this made me better at
  • Navigating mid-project pivots without losing structural integrity. The IA changed completely, but the principles I'd set — progressive disclosure, one job per page — survived the rewrite.
  • Defending architecture decisions with pattern consistency. "This breaks the pattern" became a stronger argument than "this isn't best practice."
  • Owning the IA through CMS implementation. Staying involved through Drupal taxonomy setup meant the structure survived handoff intact.
